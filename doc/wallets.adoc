[#crypto-wallets]
== Wallets

A *cryptocurrency wallet* holds the keys that control funds on a blockchain:
it makes them, keeps them secret, and signs the transactions that spend the
funds (link:https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_wallet[Wikipedia]).
Whoever holds a wallet's secret key holds its money, and a signature over the
wrong bytes spends it in a way its owner did not intend. Nothing can be
recalled: a leaked key or a wrong signature is a loss.

crypto holds the primitives a wallet needs: the key pair, the signatures, their
encodings and the tweak, each done by libsecp256k1. A blockchain's rules (BIPs,
EIPs) belong to a domain library built on top: it serializes the protocol's
messages, computes its hashes with Boost.Hash2, and calls these primitives. The
Bitcoin steps below are what such a library builds; they are not crypto's API.

[#crypto-wallets-key]
=== Making and keeping a key

*The problem.* A wallet's key must be unpredictable, must be a valid secret key
of the curve, and must survive on disk without being readable there.

*The idea.* 32 bytes from `random_device` are a valid secp256k1 secret key with
overwhelming probability; when they are not (zero, or not below the group
order), `make` says so and the wallet draws again. At rest, the secret is sealed
with AES-GCM under a key the device keeps elsewhere (a platform keystore, or a
key derived from the user's passphrase by a password hash, a hashing
library's job rather than crypto's), with the wallet's format version as
additional data.

*Mistakes to avoid.* Never derive a key from a timestamp, a counter or a
general-purpose generator. Never keep the secret in clear on disk, in a log or
in a backup. Wipe every copy of the secret once the key pair is made.

[#crypto-wallets-der]
=== An ECDSA signature in DER

*The problem.* Bitcoin's SegWit v0 inputs carry an ECDSA signature in strict
DER (link:https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0066.mediawiki[BIP 66])
over a hash the protocol defines
(link:https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0143.mediawiki[BIP 143]),
in low-S form, which Bitcoin nodes require to relay a transaction (a policy;
link:https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0146.mediawiki[BIP 146]
proposed it as a consensus rule).

*The idea.* The domain library computes the signature hash (SHA-256 twice over
the preimage it serialized); crypto signs it, deterministically and in low-S
form, and encodes the signature as strict DER. `from_der` decodes strict DER and
refuses anything else; `normalize_ecdsa` gives the low-S form of a signature
from a signer that does not enforce it.

*Mistakes to avoid.* Compare the domain library's hashes with the protocol's
published vectors: a wrong preimage signs a valid signature over the wrong
thing. Never sign with a nonce of your own: RFC 6979's deterministic nonce is
what keeps a weak generator from leaking the key.

[#crypto-wallets-taproot]
=== A tweaked key: Taproot

*The problem.* A Taproot output key is the wallet's internal key tweaked by
`t = tagged_hash("TapTweak", internal_key || merkle_root)`
(link:https://github.com/bitcoin/bips/blob/master/bip-0341.mediawiki[BIP 341]);
spending by key path signs with the tweaked key pair.

*The idea.* The tweak is the protocol's; the addition on the curve is crypto's.
The domain library computes `t` with Boost.Hash2's SHA-256 (a tagged hash is
`SHA-256(SHA-256(tag) || SHA-256(tag) || data)`) and passes it to
`tweak_xonly`, once on the public key for the address, once on the key pair to
sign:

The same two calls serve any protocol that tweaks a key: a pay-to-contract
commitment, a derived key of a hierarchy, a blinded key. crypto checks the key
and the tweak, and wipes the pair if the tweak gives no valid key; what the
tweak means is the domain library's.

*Mistakes to avoid.* A Taproot tweak is computed from public data (the internal
key and the Merkle root), so the tweaked secret key is as sensitive as the
original: anyone who knows the tweak and either secret key knows the other.
Check the domain library's tweak against BIP 341's published vectors.
